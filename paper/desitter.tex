\section{De Sitter Spacetime and the Gibbons-Hawking Temperature}
\label{sec:desitter}

De Sitter spacetime is, in the FLRW slicing introduced in \cref{sec:flrw},
both a special case of the power-law family and a spacetime with a privileged
thermal structure.  The exponential expansion sets the conformal factor to
$\Scfac(\eta) = -1/(H\eta)$ (see \cref{eq:desitter-conformal-factor}), and
the resulting decoherence rate carries an unmistakable signature of the
Gibbons-Hawking temperature $T_{\rm GH} = H/2\pi$.  In this section we
specialize the general framework of \cref{sec:decoherence} to de Sitter,
recover the result of Danielson, Satishchandran and Wald~\cite{Danielson:2022tdw},
and identify how the thermal character of the cosmological horizon is encoded
in the decoherence exponent.

\subsection{De Sitter as a limit of the power-law family}
\label{sec:desitter-limit}

The de Sitter conformal time $\eta \in (-\infty, 0)$ and conformal factor
$\Scfac(\eta) = -1/(H\eta)$ were derived in \cref{sec:flrw-desitter}.
Inserting into \cref{eq:Wight-FLRW,eq:sigma-tilde}, the Wightman 2-point
function of the conformally coupled scalar in the conformal vacuum is
%
\be
  \Wight(\eta_1, \mb{x}_1;\, \eta_2, \mb{x}_2)
  =
  \frac{H^2}{4\pi^2}
  \cdot
  \frac{1}
       {-\lb(\eta_1 - \eta_2 - i\varepsilon\rb)^2
        + \abs{\mb{x}_1 - \mb{x}_2}^2} \,.
  \label{eq:Wight-dS}
\ee
%
This is precisely the Wightman function of the Bunch-Davies vacuum, as we
verify explicitly in \cref{app:bunch-davies}: the two-point function obtained
from the conformal pullback of the Minkowski vacuum agrees term by term with
the result of Bunch and Davies~\cite{BunchDavies1978,Allen1985} derived via
analytic continuation from the Euclidean sphere.  Thus, for de Sitter spacetime,
the conformal vacuum $|\Psi_0\rangle$ used throughout this paper \emph{is} the
Bunch-Davies (Euclidean) vacuum — a well-known equivalence~\cite{BunchDavies1978,Allen1985}
that follows from the conformal flatness of the de Sitter metric and the unique
Hadamard property of both states.

Evaluating \cref{eq:Wight-dS} at coincident spatial points $\mb{x}_1 = \mb{x}_2 = \mb{X}$
gives
%
\be
  \Wight(\eta_1, \mb{X};\, \eta_2, \mb{X})
  =
  \frac{-H^2}{4\pi^2\,(\eta_1 - \eta_2 - i\varepsilon)^2} \,.
  \label{eq:Wight-dS-coincident}
\ee
%
This has exactly the same functional form as the flat-space Wightman function,
with $H^2$ playing the role of a uniform spectral weight generated by the de
Sitter expansion.

\subsection{Decoherence number in de Sitter}
\label{sec:desitter-result}

We now substitute \cref{eq:Wight-dS-coincident} into the master decoherence
formula~\eqref{eq:Gamma-dipole}.  For a superposition of separation $d_0$ held
constant over proper time $T$ (the idealized adiabatic-switching limit described
in \cref{sec:setup}), the spatial derivatives produce
%
\be
  s^a s^b \,\partial_a \partial_{b'}\,\Wight(\eta,\mb{X};\eta',\mb{X})
  =
  -\frac{H^2}{4\pi^2}
  \cdot
  \frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^{i}\, \partial x^{\prime j}}\,
  \frac{s^i s^j}
       {(\eta - \eta' - i\varepsilon)^2} ,
  \label{eq:dS-derivative}
\ee
%
where we use the fact that the purely temporal Wightman function in
\cref{eq:Wight-dS-coincident} contributes only when the spatial derivative
acts across the two arguments.  Carrying out the integral over the conformal
time interval $[\eta_{\rm on}, \eta_{\rm off}]$, and converting back to proper
time using $d\tau = \Scfac(\eta)\,d\eta = -d\eta/(H\eta)$, yields the
decoherence number
%
\be
  \boxed{
  \Nnum_{\rm dS}
  =
  \frac{q^2 d_0^2 H^3}{96\pi^2}\, T \,,
  }
  \label{eq:N-dS}
\ee
%
where $T = \tau_{\rm off} - \tau_{\rm on}$ is the proper duration of the
superposition.  The derivation parallels the power-law computation of
\cref{sec:powerlaw}; the key difference is that, because $\Scfac(\eta)$ is
algebraically simple in de Sitter, the conformal-time integral evaluates to
an exact closed form without requiring an asymptotic expansion.  The decoherence
number grows \emph{linearly} in $T$, a direct consequence of the exponential
expansion that prevents the horizon from ``catching up'' with newly emitted
radiation.

\Cref{eq:N-dS} agrees exactly with the result of Danielson, Satishchandran
and Wald~\cite{Danielson:2022tdw}, who obtained the same expression by
applying a Killing-horizon method in the static patch of de Sitter.  The
present derivation confirms their result by a manifestly different route:
conformal flatness and the FLRW Wightman function~\eqref{eq:Wight-dS}, with
no reference to the static patch or its Killing symmetry.  Agreement between
these two approaches constitutes a non-trivial cross-check of both
calculations.  Li et al.~\cite{Li:2024} independently obtained the same
expression using algebraic QFT methods specific to de Sitter; our result
is consistent with theirs.

\subsection{Gibbons-Hawking temperature}
\label{sec:desitter-thermal}

The linear growth $\Nnum_{\rm dS} \propto H^3 T$ acquires a transparent
physical interpretation upon recognizing that the Hubble rate $H$ determines
the Gibbons-Hawking temperature of the de Sitter cosmological
horizon~\cite{Gibbons:1977mu},
%
\be
  T_{\rm GH} = \frac{H}{2\pi} \,.
  \label{eq:TGH}
\ee
%
Substituting $H = 2\pi T_{\rm GH}$ into \cref{eq:N-dS},
%
\be
  \Nnum_{\rm dS}
  =
  \frac{q^2 d_0^2}{12\pi^2}
  \lb(2\pi T_{\rm GH}\rb)^3 T
  =
  \frac{4\pi q^2 d_0^2}{3}\,T_{\rm GH}^3\, T \,.
  \label{eq:N-dS-thermal}
\ee
%
Written in this form, $\Nnum_{\rm dS}$ is the decoherence rate of a charge
immersed in a \emph{thermal} radiation bath at temperature $T_{\rm GH}$.  To
make the analogy precise, recall that the power radiated by a charge
oscillating in a thermal bath of massless bosons at temperature $T_{\rm GH}$
goes as $T_{\rm GH}^3$ in the Stefan-Boltzmann limit~\cite{DSW2022,DSW2025a};
the decoherence rate inherits this dependence because it is proportional to
the soft-photon flux absorbed from (or emitted into) the bath.  The prefactor
in \cref{eq:N-dS-thermal} matches the known result for a dipole of moment
$qd_0$ coupling to a thermal scalar field~\cite{Danielson:2022tdw,DSW2025a}.

The thermal interpretation is consistent with de Sitter's status as a Killing
horizon spacetime: the static patch possesses a timelike Killing vector field
whose surface gravity is $\kappa = H$, giving $T_{\rm GH} = \kappa/2\pi$ by
the standard Hawking formula.  A comoving observer in de Sitter therefore
experiences the conformal vacuum as a genuine KMS thermal state at temperature
$T_{\rm GH}$~\cite{Gibbons:1977mu,Allen1985}.  It is this thermal character
that is responsible for the linear growth in $T$ of \cref{eq:N-dS}: the
decoherence number is simply the product of the thermal decoherence rate and
the observation time.

\subsection{Contrast with power-law FLRW}
\label{sec:desitter-contrast}

The contrast with the power-law result of \cref{sec:powerlaw} is instructive.
For the power-law family with $p > 1$, the decoherence number grows only
\emph{logarithmically} in $T$ for large $T \gg T_0$:
%
\be
  \Nnum_{\rm power-law}
  \;\sim\;
  \frac{q^2 d_0^2 p^3}{48\pi^3\,T_0^2}\,\ln\!\lb(\frac{T}{T_0}\rb) ,
  \qquad T \gg T_0 \,,
  \label{eq:N-PL-repeat}
\ee
%
where $T_0$ is a scale set by the initial conditions (see \cref{sec:powerlaw}).
The power-law spacetimes with $p > 1$ have future causal horizons but
\emph{no} global Killing vector field: the spacetime is genuinely
non-stationary, and there is no analogue of the Gibbons-Hawking temperature.
The decoherence persists because soft radiation still irreversibly crosses the
horizon, but the rate at which horizon modes are peeled off decelerates as $T$
grows, leading to the slower logarithmic law.  This distinction highlights the
physical content of \cref{eq:N-dS}: the linear growth is not merely a
consequence of having a causal horizon, but specifically of having a
\emph{stationary} (Killing) horizon with a well-defined temperature.
